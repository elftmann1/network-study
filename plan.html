The weekly rhythm

This is the actual answer to your question. Labs are not a separate activity you bolt on at the end — they belong in the same week as the videos they apply to, and quizzing belongs one week behind the videos, so you're recalling from memory rather than from short-term echo.

SlotTimeWhat you do
Sessions 1–3 4.5 hrs Watch this week's videos. Pause and write your own notes by hand — not a transcript, just what you'd need to re-explain it. Don't watch at 2×; you're a beginner, and the vocabulary is the hard part.
Session 4 2.5 hrs Lab this week's material. Same week, while the vocabulary is fresh. Build it, break it, watch it fail, fix it. If a lab works on the first try you learned less than if it didn't.
Session 5 1.5 hrs Retrieval on last week's topics. Flashcards, closed-notes questions, or explain it out loud. Feeling like you've forgotten it is the point — that's the reps that stick.
Session 6 1 hr Weak spots + ports drill. Re-watch the one video that didn't land. Run your ports deck. Do the day's Professor Messer pop quiz.
Every session 0.5 hr Warm-up: five subnetting problems and one ports round. From week 3 onward, every single session, no exceptions. These two topics are pure speed and they carry disproportionate weight on the exam.

Your free toolkit

Set all of this up in week 1 so tooling never becomes the reason you skip a lab.

Primary lab

Cisco Packet Tracer

Free with a Cisco Networking Academy account. Simulates switches, routers, servers, and wireless controllers. This carries roughly 80% of your labs and is the best free rehearsal for the exam's performance-based questions.

Packet inspection

Wireshark

Free. Lets you see the frames and headers Messer draws on screen actually arriving on your own machine. Turns the OSI model from a mnemonic into something observable.

Command line

A Linux VM

VirtualBox plus any Ubuntu ISO, both free. Objective 5.5 expects dig, tcpdump, ip, and netstat — you want somewhere to actually run them.

Retrieval

Anki

Free on desktop. Build your own ports, connectors, and protocols decks rather than downloading one — writing the card is half the learning, and shared decks are often built for the older N10-008 objectives.

Questions

Messer pop quizzes & study groups

Free on professormesser.com. Daily N10-009 pop quizzes plus monthly live study groups with replays. The closest free thing to exam-style questions written for this exact objective set.

Questions

CompTIA's sample questions

Free on comptia.org. A small set, but written by the people who write the exam — useful mainly for calibrating how questions are phrased.

Hardware

Your own home network

Free, and underrated. Log into your router. Read its DHCP pool, its NAT table, its wireless channel. Objectives 1.5, 2.3, and 3.4 are all sitting in that admin page.

Hardware

A WiFi analyzer app

Free on Android; iOS options are more limited. Walk your home doing a channel survey — it makes objective 5.4's wireless interference troubleshooting concrete instead of abstract.

Where a free-only plan is weakest

Practice exams

Everything else in this plan is genuinely well covered by free tools. Full-length, well-written N10-009 practice exams are the exception. Most free question banks online were written for the retired N10-008 objectives, and a fair number are scraped exam content — using those violates CompTIA's candidate agreement and can get a certification revoked, so avoid anything advertising "real exam questions" or "dumps."

What free material actually gives you is question practice, not scored simulations: Messer's pop quizzes and study groups, CompTIA's sample set, and your own closed-book self-testing. That's enough to learn the material. It's thinner for calibrating whether you're ready.

So if you ever decide to spend anything at all on this, one legitimate practice-exam set is the highest-value purchase available — higher than any book or video course, because you already have those for free. Buy it around week 8, when you have enough breadth for the score to mean something. Skipping it isn't a dealbreaker; it just means leaning harder on the week 11 break-and-fix labs to judge your own readiness.
